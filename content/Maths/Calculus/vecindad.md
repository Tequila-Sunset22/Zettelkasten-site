An open interval that contains a point $x$ is said to be a **neighborhood** of $x$. Thus each point in $(a,b)$ possesses a neighborhood, indeed many neighborhoods, that lie entirely inside the set. We say $(c,d)$ is a neighborhood of $x$ if $x$ belongs to the interval. We say that the set $(c,d) \setminus \{ x \}$ is a **deleted neighborhood**.
 
We can distinguish between points that are merely in a set and points that are more deeply inside the set. The word chosen to convey this image of "inside" is **interior**.<sup><a href="#user-content-fn-inline-1" id="user-content-fnref-inline-1" data-footnote-ref class="internal">1</a></sup>

> [!info]
>
> Sea $\varepsilon >0$, definiendo $(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)$ evaluamos la métrica dentro del intervalo, con algún otro punto interior $x_{0}$, de la forma
> $$
> \begin{align}
> x - \varepsilon < x_{0} < x + \varepsilon \\[1em]
> -\varepsilon < x_{0}-x < \varepsilon \\[1em]
> \lvert x-x_{0} \rvert < \varepsilon
> \end{align}
> $$
